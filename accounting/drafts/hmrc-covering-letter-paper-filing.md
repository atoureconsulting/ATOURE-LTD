Dear Sir/Madam,

**Company Tax Returns filed by paper — company tax reference 2411414574**

I am writing on behalf of Atoure Ltd to enclose three completed Company
Tax Returns (form CT600), with accounts and computations attached to
each, for the following accounting periods:

- 11 September 2023 to 10 September 2024
- 11 September 2024 to 30 September 2024
- 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025

I am aware that HMRC's free online Company Tax Return service closed on
31 March 2026, and that commercial filing software is now the normal
route for delivering a return electronically. I am filing these three
returns on paper because I have not yet been able to secure commercial
filing software: I am the sole director of Atoure Ltd, I applied for a
free business account that provides access to compatible software
(Mettle, which provides free FreeAgent access) shortly before this
letter, and that application remains under review at the time of
writing. Given the filing deadline for the most recent of these three
periods falls on 30 September 2026, I have chosen to file on paper now,
using figures I have prepared and verified directly against the
company's bank records, rather than risk a further missed deadline
while that software access is pending.

This also follows on from my letter of 17 September 2026 appealing the
late filing penalties already charged for the first two periods above,
in which I explained that I was hospitalised as an emergency admission
from 7 to 25 September 2024 with diabetic ketoacidosis and a new
diagnosis of diabetes mellitus, and was unable to attend to the
company's administrative affairs, including its filing obligations,
during that admission and for a period of recovery afterwards. As the
company's sole director, with no other officer, employee, or agent able
to act on its behalf, this also delayed putting the company's filing
arrangements — including its software access — on a proper footing,
which is part of why paper filing is still necessary for these returns
now.

The figures in each return are drawn directly from the company's bank
statements (Santander, Revolut Business, and Revolut Personal) and
supersede the amounts HMRC assumed in the determinations issued on
2 September 2026 for the first two periods, which I understand were
raised in the absence of a return and used the main rate of Corporation
Tax rather than the small profits rate that actually applies. The
Corporation Tax self-assessed as payable in each return is:

- 11 September 2023 to 10 September 2024: £474.65
- 11 September 2024 to 30 September 2024: £7.86
- 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025: £553.14

I will arrange payment of these amounts, together with interest for
late payment where applicable, separately from this letter. I would be
grateful if these returns could be processed as soon as possible so
that the determinations already issued can be superseded and any
associated collection action can be updated accordingly.

Please let me know if any further information is required.

Yours faithfully,

Abdul-Malik Baba Toure
Director, Atoure Ltd

## Notes on this draft

- **Purpose:** this is not an appeal (that's the separate penalty-appeal
  letter already sent 17 September 2026) — it's a plain explanation of
  *why* these returns are arriving on paper, so HMRC processes them
  without querying the filing method, and so there's a clear paper trail
  if the paper-filing "reasonable excuse" basis is ever questioned.
- **Timing:** best sent in the same envelope/parcel as the three CT600
  forms and their accounts/computations, so HMRC has the context
  immediately on opening it.
- **If Mettle/FreeAgent access comes through before you post this:**
  consider filing electronically instead where still possible — paper
  filing should be the fallback, not the default, once software access
  is confirmed. Check the actual filing deadline situation for A01/A02
  (already overdue, no online exemption issue there) versus Period 3
  (due 30 September 2026 — filing this one on paper only if software
  access won't arrive in time).
- **Company name on HMRC's own correspondence** appears as "ATOURE LTD"
  (their internal formatting/caps) — the legal registered name at
  Companies House is "Atoure Ltd", used consistently in this letter and
  the CT600 forms.
