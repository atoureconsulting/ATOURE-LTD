**Appeal against flat-rate late filing penalty determination — Company

I am writing on behalf of Atoure Ltd to appeal against the £1,000
flat-rate penalty determination issued on 2 November 2026 for the
period 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025.

I understand this penalty reflects the higher flat-rate tier applied
when a company's return is late for a third accounting period in
succession. I would ask HMRC to look at why all three periods were
late together, rather than treating this one in isolation, because the
lateness of this third period flows directly from the same underlying
cause as the first two.

As explained in my earlier appeal (17 September 2026, regarding periods
11 September 2023 to 10 September 2024 and 11 September 2024 to
30 September 2024), I was admitted as an emergency to the Royal Free
Hospital on 7 September 2024 with diabetic ketoacidosis and a new
diagnosis of diabetes mellitus, remained an inpatient including a
period in intensive care until 25 September 2024, and needed a further
period of recovery afterwards. As the company's sole director, with no
other officer, employee, or agent able to act on its behalf, this left
the company's filing arrangements in a backlog that I have been working
through steadily ever since, rather than something I was able to
resolve all at once.

By the time I was in a position to address the backlog, HMRC's free
online Company Tax Return service had closed (31 March 2026), making
commercial filing software mandatory. Sourcing and setting up suitable
software took time: I researched and selected a provider from HMRC's
own list of recognised commercial suppliers, and then had to resolve a
Government Gateway authentication error that blocked submission for
several days despite my credentials being confirmed valid directly on
gov.uk. I used this same software to file all three outstanding
returns as soon as each one was ready, working through them in period
order, rather than leaving any of them outstanding any longer than
necessary.

The return for this period (1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025) was
filed only a short time after the normal 30 September 2026 deadline,
once the software access and authentication issues above were
resolved, and once the first two periods — which were further behind
and needed resolving first — were filed. It was not a fresh, separate
failure: it was the tail end of the same recovery process set out
above, affecting a sole director with no one else able to act for the
company in the meantime.

I would be grateful if HMRC would reconsider the £1,000 flat-rate
penalty in light of this, either by reducing it to reflect that the
underlying cause was a continuous reasonable excuse rather than three
unrelated defaults, or by cancelling it. In the meantime, I would ask
that collection of this penalty be suspended pending the outcome of
this appeal.

Please let me know if any further information or supporting
documentation would help.

Yours faithfully,

Director, Atoure Ltd

## Notes on this draft

- **Why this argument, not a fresh/separate excuse:** the £1,000 figure
  is the escalated "persistent default" tier (flat-rate penalties
  double to £500+£500 when a return is late for the third successive
  period) rather than the normal £100+£100 cap. The strongest argument
  is therefore not "Period 3 itself had its own emergency" (it didn't —
  the hospitalisation was Sept 2024) but that the *persistence* HMRC is
  penalising is itself the direct, continuous consequence of the
  already-accepted reasonable excuse for A01/A02, compounded by the
  mandatory switch to commercial software and a genuine authentication
  fault (Error 1046) that was outside the user's control.
- **Ties to the first appeal:** explicitly cross-references the
  17 September 2026 letter rather than repeating all the medical detail
  — avoids duplicating the NHS evidence while keeping the chain of
  causation clear. If HMRC wants the NHS documents again they're
  already on file from the first appeal.
- **Lateness was short:** the return was filed only a few weeks after
  the 30 September 2026 deadline, and voluntarily (not prompted by a
  further determination) — worth emphasising this isn't a case of
  prolonged non-compliance once the backlog was actually reachable.
- **Risk:** this appeal does not address — and does not need to
  address — the separate tax-related penalty or interest for Period 3,
  which are not yet known. A separate appeal/reconsideration may be
  needed once those letters arrive, same as happened for A01/A02.
- **Still outstanding:** print, review, sign (or use the pre-signed
  PDF), and post by **2 December 2026**.
